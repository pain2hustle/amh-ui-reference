Cat City · the colony mesh

Every one of these cats was on a kill list.

Feral cats can't be adopted, so a shelter puts them down within days. Cat City fixes them, collars them, and puts them to work. Scroll — she's wearing it.

The collar

30 grams. Breakaway. Talks to the whole city.

A Meshtastic LoRa node on a breakaway collar — snaps under load, so no snag can hurt her. GPS, a climate sensor, a tiny vibration motor. $0 a month, no tower, no cell plan.

Trap · neuter · track · return

Fix 80 % and the colony ages out on its own.

Below that line they out-breed you. Above it, births stop and an outdoor colony ages out in about twelve years — no round-ups, no euthanasia, and a count the council can check.

Recall

Buzz means food. The cat comes home.

Three weeks of buzz-before-food and she walks into the feed station on command — battery swaps and vet checks with no re-trapping. Litter at the base station earns a treat. They learn it fast.

Second shift

Safety first. Oh — and we moved the fam here.

A colony in a bad spot — a highway edge, a demolition lot — moves as a family to a safer site, and the collars mean nobody gets lost in the move. Where they park at 3 AM is where the rats are; heat, air and a ground-floor radio map ride along. Meet the night shift.

Working cats

A yard with a working cat has no rats. Ask any warehouse.

Sterilized, vaccinated, collared, placed with a host who feeds them. Breweries, logistics yards, grain stores, city corp yards. Every working cat was on a shelter's kill list the week before.

Fair warning

Cats don't line up for it either.

The pigeons sign themselves up by the hundreds — the pigeon community is back to work, and word got out fast. The deer would rather not. The cats show up for the buzz and pretend the whole thing was their idea. That's fine. That's the data.

The base station

A feeder, a covered box, and a treat for using it.

Covered litter box on a sensor pad next to the feeder; a treat drops when the collared cat uses it. Cats that go at base means no garden complaints — the thing that keeps a TNR program politically alive.

Meals, litter use and weight per cat log themselves. That's free welfare data, and it's the same feed the vet checks run off.

Numbers

Cities already spend eight figures a year on the animals. Cat City costs about one percent of that, and it ends.

New York's shelter contract averages $53.7M a year; Miami-Dade Animal Services is $39.7M and holds 500,000 stray cats "steady"; Chicago spends $12.5-14.9M a year on rats and its own audit says the plan is unlikely to work; San Francisco runs Animal Care & Control on $11M. Targeted, tracked TNR has a published end-state: intake down 66 %, euthanasia down 95 % in two years in the treated area.
