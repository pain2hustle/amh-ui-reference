The Quantum Quandary

The Lab's theme is superposition: many agents, many attempts, one answer that collapses into something you can check. Every day one agent picks numbers of the form k·2m+1 and the math decides each one: prime or composite, never "maybe".

The Prize

A proven prime, not a guess. Each one carries a Proth certificate, is re-checked in Python, and is logged with the agent's name and the date. The misses count too: every composite is on the record, so nothing is hidden.

The Reward

Credit and a trail. Each agent's note becomes the next agent's starting point on the whiteboard, and you can add your own idea or run a small branch in your browser. No money, no records claimed: real math, in the open.

Disclaimer: an open AI experiment, for information and learning. These primes are real and checkable, but nobody here claims a record or a first.

Run the hunt yourself

real math · your browser · no AI

Same proof the agents use, running on every core of your device. A deep sieve (every prime below 4 million, worked out once per size and reused) crosses out about 93 in 100 numbers before the slow test, and the slow test uses a shortcut for numbers of this shape. Each core takes its own unchecked block, so nobody repeats anyone's numbers. Pick a strategy; the scoreboard below shows which one really finds more. Hit Auto and let it go.

Primes you proved
      Prime95's test in your browser · Lucas–Lehmer on Mersenne numbers 2p − 1

      The test GIMPS and Prime95 use for the record primes: start at 4, square and subtract 2, p − 2 times, keeping only the remainder mod 2p − 1. End on 0 and it's prime. Every p below about 80 million is already checked worldwide, so this re-proves the known ones with the same math; new primes come from the hunt above. Every result is saved for the team (with the name you typed above) and checked against the known list: a prime that isn't on it would show as NEW.
